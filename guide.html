Getting started

  1. Sign in with your email. There's no password: the app emails you a sign-in code. Type it in (or tap the link in the email).
  2. Join your team, or set up your brewery. If your admin invited the email you signed in with, you join automatically. If not, the app asks whether your brewery already uses Brewery OS:
    • Yes, I'm joining my team: type the join code from your invite email (like grist-knockout-4821; capitals and spaces don't matter). The code works with any email, so it's the answer when you were invited at one address and signed in with another. No code? Ask your admin to invite the email you signed in with, then tap Check for invites.
    • No, I'm setting it up: name your brewery and you're its admin. If anyone at your brewery already uses Brewery OS, ask them to invite you instead, so you're all looking at the same tanks.
    Made a brewery by mistake? Delete it in Settings → Brewery.
  3. Set your units (°F or °C; Plato, SG, or Brix; barrels, hectoliters, or gallons) in Settings → Brewery. Everything is shown and typed in your units.
  4. Add your locations and tanks in Settings → Equipment, or bring them in from a spreadsheet with Settings → Import. (A brand-new brewery's empty tank board also offers Sample data to try things out, or a backup file.)
  5. Add your beers in Settings → Beers, and start batches from the tank board.
  6. Invite your crew in Settings → Team and permissions.

Everything has sensible defaults, so you can start using the tank board right away and set up the rest (brew sheet fields, package types, alerts) when you get to it.

The demo

Try the demo on the sign-in screen opens a demo brewery of your own, with no email: two locations (a 15 bbl and a 30 bbl brewhouse, so batches of one, two, and three turns), about three months of made-up brewing ending today, and every part of the app in use: brew sheets, cellar logs, inventory, raw materials, the calendar, and menu boards.

  • A two-minute tour starts the first time a demo opens: it walks through the tank board, alerts, a batch's story and its brew-day sheet (turn by turn), inventory and pars, raw materials, the calendar, and a menu board, on the real screens with the demo's own records. Back, Next, and Skip (or ←, →, and Esc); Take the tour on the yellow bar plays it again.
  • It's yours alone. Change anything; nobody else sees it. Coming back on the same phone or computer brings you back to it, until it's deleted a week after it was made (the yellow bar says when).
  • Switched off in the demo: invites, API keys, calendar links, and uploading fonts (nothing in a demo sends an email or lasts beyond it). Menu board links work, so a demo's board can be shown on a TV.
  • A demo never becomes a real brewery. Start your own brewery signs you out of the demo (for good: with no email, there's no way back to it), so you can sign in with your email and set up your own.

The tank board

The first screen: every tank, grouped by location. A full tank shows its beer, batch number, how much is in it, its stage (with its color), and how many days it's been in that stage. An empty tank shows its status: Empty, Cleaning, or Maintenance, and Acid due (with why) when it needs an acid cycle before it's filled.

  • Tap a full tank to open the batch's page (see A batch's page).
  • Tap an empty tank to start a batch in it (if you're allowed to), or to change its status.
  • Alerts (the orange box at the top) list what needs attention; see Alerts.
  • Changes to review (a dashed box, when there are any): changes an API key suggested, waiting for someone to Approve or Reject; see Settings: API keys.
  • Packaged batches (below the tanks) lists batches that are out of the tanks: packaged, or used in another batch. Tap one to open it.
  • Inventory and Settings are at the top.

Messages show in a bar at the top (tap × to close one), and questions like "Delete FV2?" in a box that says what the button does: Enter answers yes, Esc answers no.

A red bar means no signal: you're seeing the last saved copy, and changes are kept on the phone (see No signal). A green bar, "A new version of the app is ready", means the app was updated: tap Reload when you're not in the middle of typing.

Starting and moving batches

Start a batch

Tap an empty tank. Enter the batch number (it must be unique), the beer, the brew date, and the size. It starts Fermenting unless you choose another stage. A new batch gets the location's usual number of turns (see brewhouse settings).

Change the stage or move it

On the batch's page, tap Stage / transfer…. The stages are Fermenting, Dry hopping, Conditioning, Carbonating, Ready, and Packaged. When you pick a new stage, its start date is set to today (you can change it).

  • To transfer, pick the new tank. The form asks for the volume moved; leave it empty for "all of it". If you move less than was in the tank, what's left behind (yeast, trub) is recorded as a loss, and the form tells you before you save. The old tank goes to Cleaning.
  • The app warns (but lets you continue) when the new tank is being cleaned, needs acid, or is too small, and refuses a tank that already holds another batch.
  • Undo: after you start a batch, change its stage, transfer it, package it, or make a split or blend, a message at the top says what was saved, with Undo for 8 seconds. Undo takes it back before it's sent, so the records never see it. (Other changes wait those few seconds too, so everything still arrives in order.) After that, change it back by hand.
  • Packaged here means the whole batch is out of the tank. To count kegs and cases, use Package… instead (see Packaging).

Every change is kept in the batch's history; a mistake is fixed by saving the right values, and the history shows both.

A batch's page

Tap a full tank. At the top: the stage, the tank, days in the stage, how much is in the tank, and the brew date. Then:

  • The numbers: OG (from the tank sample on the brew-day sheet, or the average of the turns' knockout gravities), Now (the latest gravity; FG once the batch is past fermenting), ABV, and attenuation, each next to the beer's target. They're worked out from the readings, so fixing a reading fixes them.
  • In stock / Made from / Part went into: finished goods from this batch still on hand, and the batches it was split from or blended into (each a link).
  • Buttons: + Log cellar work, + Addition, Stage / transfer…, Brew-day sheet, Print brew sheet, Package…, Check level, Split / blend… (each shows only if you're allowed to use it).
  • Fermentation chart: gravity and temperature by day since brewing, with the target FG as a dashed line. It appears once there are a couple of readings. Under it, when the batch has a couple of pH readings, a pH strip on the same days (starting from the brew-day sheet's knockout pH), so a check's pH sits right under its gravity: a healthy fermentation's pH drops in the first days and levels off.
  • Cellar log: what was done and measured, newest first.
  • Ingredients and additions: brew-day ingredients (tagged "brew day") and cellar additions like dry hops, with lot numbers.
  • History: every stage change, transfer, and volume movement, oldest first.

Log cellar work

Tap + Log cellar work: the date, an action (Check, Tank sample, Dry hop, Rouse, Crash, Harvest, Drain, Spund, Carbonate, Ready, Other), gravity, pH, temperature, a cellar change ("FR to 62"), and notes. Dry hop, Crash, Carbonate, and Ready offer to move the batch to the matching stage (Dry hopping, Conditioning, Carbonating, Ready) in the same step. Tap an entry to correct it (it's marked "edited") or delete it.

Additions

Tap + Addition for dry hops, fruit, spices and the like: what, how much, when, and the lot number. The name and lot suggestions come from your raw materials.

The brew-day sheet

On a batch's page, tap Brew-day sheet. It follows your paper sheet's order (choose the fields in Settings → Brew sheet).

  • Each value saves as soon as you leave its box, also with no signal.
  • Turns are tabs (Turn 1, Turn 2...). Fields marked "whole batch" (yeast, knockout volume, notes) are shared by all turns. Change the number of turns with the Turns box.
  • Targets show under each field: fixed ones (sparge temperature, pH ranges), your brewhouse's (flow, kettle full), the beer's OG, and the water worked out from the grist weight. A value far from its target is highlighted ("Typo?"); how far is set in Settings → Brewery.
  • Water: once the grist weight is in, the sheet shows the mash, sparge, and total water for that turn: mash = grist × water-to-grist ÷ 4; total = kettle full × 31 + grist × grain absorption; sparge = total − mash (gallons; set the numbers in the location's brewhouse settings).
  • Flow meter fields take the start and end readings and save the difference.
  • Ingredients are at the top: + Ingredient (kind, amount, when, turn, lot number), or copy them in one tap from the last batch of this beer or from a recipe (lot numbers start empty; dry hops and other cellar additions aren't copied, so they're logged when they go in).
  • Correcting a value keeps the earlier one in the database's history.

Your permission level needs "Start batches and edit batch details" to fill it in; others can see it.

The printed brew sheet

On a batch's page, tap Print brew sheet (or Print on the brew-day sheet). It prints the same fields in the same order with a box for each turn, the targets (water as the formula, since the grist isn't weighed yet), the ingredients with a Lot column and blank rows, and whatever's already been entered. The usual set of fields fits on one Letter page.

The QR code opens that batch in the app (after signing in) to type in the numbers. With no signal, the link is printed instead.

Volumes and level checks

Every tank shows how much is in it. The app doesn't store that number: it works it out from every movement of beer, like a bank balance: the knockout into a fermenter, transfers, packaging, losses, and level checks. So a mistake is fixed with a new record, never by editing a number, and every barrel can be traced.

  • Knockout volume comes from the brew-day sheet's "Total knockout volume", or the batch size if that's empty.
  • Volume not recorded shows when no volume was ever entered; everything still works, and a level check sets it.

Check level (sight glass)

On a batch's page, tap Check level and type what the sight glass shows. If it's less than on record, say what the difference was: served (poured from this tank; the default for serving tanks), loss, or a correction. More than on record is noted as a correction. Later movements count from the latest check.

Packaging

On a batch's page, tap Package…. Enter how many of each package type you filled (set your types in Settings → Packages). As you type, the app shows the barrels, about how much is left in the tank, and what's left would fill ("about 20 × ½ bbl keg").

  • Into: the stock place the packages go to (the tank location's storage place, unless you choose another).
  • Is the tank empty now? Not yet keeps the rest for another run. Yes, it's spent records what's left on paper as a loss, marks the batch packaged, and sends the tank to Cleaning. If more than 10% would be left over, the app asks first.
  • Each run keeps the volume per package it was packaged with, so changing a package type later never changes a past run.

Splits and blends

On a batch's page, tap Split / blend…. Both make a new batch, so a tank always holds one batch and each part has its own stage, additions, and packaging.

  • Split: say how much of this batch goes into the new one, and the tank. The source keeps the rest.
  • Blend: add other batches with + Blend in another batch…, each with how much.
  • All used: ticked when you take all of a source (empty "how much"), unticked when you type a part (change it either way). A source that's all used becomes "Used in another batch": what's left is a loss, and its tank goes to Cleaning. A blend can go into one of its sources' tanks if that source is all used.
  • The suggested number is "142-2" for a split and "142/143" for a blend. The new batch's OG is worked out from its sources, by volume.

Inventory: finished goods

Tap Inventory on the tank board. Kegs and cases on hand, worked out from records: packaging puts them in, removals and counts take them out.

  • Places: choose a place (each location's Storage, a Taproom...) or All places. Add places in Settings → Equipment.
  • Views (the dashed chips) are your own sheets, like a master sheet: which places they add up, a column per size or per place and size, totals, brewery-wide pars, and the pipeline (beer still in tanks). + View makes one; Edit view changes it.
  • Order: each place lists beers A–Z, oldest batch first, in its own order (Arrange…), or (a taproom) by draft line.
  • Tap a beer to see the batches behind it, oldest first ("From before the app" for opening counts).

Count, move, remove

  • Count a place: a count sheet that starts at what the app expects; change what's different. Less than expected at a taproom is recorded as poured; elsewhere you choose (sold, not sure, dumped). Add a beer to count beer from before the app (opening stock).
  • Move: from one place to another (up to the taproom, to the other location).
  • Remove: sold, taproom, transferred (in bond), donated or samples, or dumped, with an account name.
  • Taking stock out always uses the oldest batch first.
  • Reasons ("Stocked the taproom", "Dock sale"): your own list, offered on every count, move, and removal, and required if your admin turned that on (Settings → Packages).

Pars, restocking, draft lines, and on deck

  • Set pars on a place (barrels and/or cases); with All places, the brewery-wide par. Each place shows over / under, and what to bring up ("Bring up 3 × ½ bbl keg from Storage"; Bring up fills in the move).
  • Draft lines (a taproom): numbered lines pouring a beer, something else (wine, a guest beer), empty, or out of order. Tap a line to change it. Count sheets for a taproom follow the lines.
  • On deck (a taproom): beers in storage that aren't on a line or on hand there yet.

Inventory: raw materials

Inventory → Raw materials. On hand by lot = received − used on batches + counts. Receiving, counting, ordering, and setting up items need their own permission, "Receive, count, and order raw materials" (separate from finished goods).

  • Items: malt, hops, salts, yeast, chemicals, each with a unit, a pack (a 55 lb sack), a reorder level, and (optionally) how many days it takes to arrive when ordered. The item's name is what's typed as an ingredient, so usage is counted automatically.
  • Receive: a delivery by lot, in packs or the item's unit, with the supplier and (optionally) the cost.
  • Used up automatically by brew-day ingredients and cellar additions with the same name and lot (lb, kg, oz, and g convert; so do gal, L, and mL).
  • Count: what's actually on the shelf for a lot; the difference is recorded with a reason.
  • On order: a delivery you're expecting (item, how much, the day it's due, the supplier). It counts toward brews planned after that day on the calendar, so an ordered shortfall stops warning. When it comes, Received opens the delivery form already filled in (add the lot number); a delivery past its day is marked. Cancel takes it off.
  • Below the reorder level is flagged. Tap an item for its lots and the batches that used each one.

Planning calendar

Calendar on the tank board opens the plan: tanks down the side (and a Whole brewery row for things not tied to a tank, like a delivery), days across the top. A phone shows three days, a computer the whole week; ‹ and › move, Today comes back. A plan is never a record: nothing on the calendar changes a batch, a tank, or stock. Doing the work records it, as usual.

  • Plan something: tap a square (it fills in that tank and day) or + Plan. Choose what (Brew day, Dry hop, Diacetyl rest, Crash, Transfer, Carbonate, Package, Clean, Acid cycle, Yeast harvest, Maintenance, Delivery, or Other), optionally a name in your words, the beer, and notes.
  • Someday: instead of a day, say roughly when ("Fall"). Someday plans are listed above the week; give one a day when it's real.
  • Beer schedules: each beer's steps in days after brewing (dry hop on day 5, package on day 12...). The calendar lays them out, drawn lighter as expected, after a planned brew and for batches already in tanks. Tap an expected item to open its batch (or the planned brew it comes from).
  • Gravity steps: a step can also go off at a gravity ("dry hop at 4 °P", for dry hop, crash, carbonate, transfer, and package). It sits on its day, labeled "at 4.0 °P"; when a logged reading reaches it, it's due now on today (and the Gravity step due alert), until the records show it's done. Under Beer schedules, choose whether one reading at or below it is enough, or two in a row. Always a reading someone logged, never a forecast.
  • Suggest from past batches (in Beer schedules): fills in each step from how many days after brewing it really happened, on average, in this beer's past batches. Check them, then save.
  • Clashes, before they happen: a planned brew is marked ⚠ with a red border when its tank won't be free: a batch is still in it (until its planned or expected transfer or packaging), another brew is planned there first, it's in maintenance, or (for a brew in the next week) it needs an acid cycle. Tap it to see why.
  • Late, and pushing the rest back: when a batch is behind its beer's schedule (still not dry hopping on its dry hop day), that step shows on today, marked late. Tap it to push the batch's remaining steps back (it suggests the days it's behind). If that would clash with a brew planned in its tank, it says so and asks first. The app tells a step is done from the records: dry hopping (the stage, or a dry hop addition), crashed (Conditioning or later), carbonating, moved out of its first tank, or packaged; steps it can't tell (a diacetyl rest) simply pass. A planned item left undone shows on today as late, too, until it's moved or ticked done.
  • Will there be enough? A planned brew (with its beer and tank) needs its beer's recipe, written for one turn times the brewhouse's usual turns (or for the whole batch: Settings → Beers), or, with no recipe, what the last batch of the beer used. Going through the planned brews in date order: on hand, plus deliveries on order due by then, minus each earlier brew. A brew that comes up short is marked short; tap it to see how much of what, in packs. Recipe ingredients count only when a raw material has the same name; the rest are listed, never guessed.
  • Dismiss a shortfall someone has dealt with ("borrowing 2 sacks from next door"): it stops alerting, and comes back if the shortfall gets bigger.
  • Shopping list: everything the brews in the look-ahead (two weeks to start; longer for items that take longer to arrive) will be short of, how much, in packs, and when to order by. On order… on a line records the order; Copy copies the list. The Short for a planned brew alert emails the same (see Settings: Alerts).
  • Move or tick done: tap an item to change its day or tank, or tick Done. A planned brew counts as done once a batch of that beer is started in that tank within a few days of it.
  • Who: an item can name a person from the team ("Sam brews Tuesday"); it shows on the item. My week shows only what you're on (tap again for everything).
  • From your planning sheet: bring the plan in with Settings → Import (Calendar plan).
  • Print: the week shown, Monday to Sunday, on one landscape page for the brewhouse wall: what's planned (and who), what's expected, and clashes (⚠).
  • In Google or Apple Calendar: a private, read-only link in Settings → My account.
  • Who can do what: everyone sees the calendar. Plan the schedule (head brewers and admins, to start) adds, changes, and deletes, and sets beer schedules; Move items (brewers too) moves items, ticks them done, and says who's on them. See Who can do what.

Alerts

The orange box on the tank board lists what needs attention: no gravity logged lately, too long in a stage, acid due, a beer under par, low on a raw material. The server checks every 15 minutes (and the app checks whenever it loads), so it's current even when nobody has the app open.

  • I've got it takes an alert off the board.
  • An alert clears by itself when its cause goes away (a gravity is logged, the tank gets its acid cycle).
  • The people chosen for each kind are emailed about each new alert once: one email per person listing everything new. Set it up in Settings → Alerts.

No signal (offline)

The app keeps working with no signal. A red bar shows how old the data on screen is. Floor work is kept on the phone and sent, in order, when the signal returns: starting, moving, and packaging batches; tank status; acid cycles; cellar log entries and additions; brew-day sheet values; level checks; splits and blends; counts, moves, and removals; raw material deliveries and counts.

Setup changes (beers, tanks, locations, settings) need a signal.

If a change can't be saved when it's sent (say, a coworker filled that tank first), it's listed in a "These changes couldn't be saved" box at the top, so nothing is lost silently. Changes arrive in the order they were made.

Settings: Brewery

  • Brewery name (admins).
  • Units and time zone: temperature (°F or °C), gravity (Plato, SG, or Brix), volume (barrels, hectoliters, or gallons). Changing a unit only changes how numbers are shown and typed; nothing recorded changes.
  • Flag brew-day values far from target: how far a value can be from its target before the brew-day sheet highlights it, for gravity, temperature, pH, and volumes (a percentage). Leave a box empty to turn that flag off.
  • Delete this brewery (made by mistake, or only tried with the sample data): shown to the admin only while they're the only person in the brewery. Type the brewery's name to confirm; it deletes everything in it and can't be undone, so download a backup first if you might want it. A brewery with a team can't be deleted here.

Settings: Equipment

  • Tanks: name, type (Fermenter, Brite tank, Serving tank, Lagering tank), capacity, location, status, and "acid every X turns" (tap a tank to change it or log an acid cycle).
  • Locations, each with its brewhouse settings for the brew-day sheet: turn size, usual turns per batch, kettle-full volume, flow target, water-to-grist ratio (qt/lb), and grain absorption (gal/lb).
  • Stock places: where finished kegs and cases are kept. Each location starts with a Storage place; add a taproom or more. Rename or hide a place (it's never deleted, since past counts point to it).

Settings: Brew sheet

  • Tick the fields you measure from a catalog of about 100, in paper order. Usual set and Everything, and All / None for each section. A batch always shows every field it has a value for, ticked or not.
  • Edit a field to rename it (your crew's term) and set its target: the usual one, your own range in your units, or none. Back to usual undoes it.
  • Add your own field: a name, the section, and the kind of value (a number with your unit, temperature, gravity, volume, pH, time, or text).

Settings: Beers and recipes

  • Beers: name, style, target OG and FG (the target ABV is worked out).
  • On the menu: what the taproom's menu says about a beer, using the pour sizes, sections, tags, and fields set up in Settings → Menu:
    • a short line for the TV (up to 80 characters) and a longer description (for print and the public page);
    • ABV, IBU, and color (SRM, shown as a swatch). Fill in ABV, IBU, and color fills the boxes from the latest finished batch's gravities (or the beer's targets) and the recipe's IBU and color, and says where each came from; check them, then Save;
    • its section, its tags, and the brewery's own fields;
    • prices: a price for each pour size it's poured in (leave the others empty). With two or more taprooms, each has its own column: a price there is used at that taproom instead; leave it empty for the usual price;
    • Leave off the public menu: still on the TV and print, but not on the menu page for the public.
    Someone with only "Beer menu details" (like the Taproom level) can change these, but not the beer itself.
  • Recipes are written for one turn (the calendar multiplies by the brewhouse's usual turns) or the whole batch.
  • Recipes: Import BeerXML… from BeerSmith, Brewfather, Brewer's Friend, or other recipe software. A preview shows each recipe (size, OG, FG, IBU, ingredients); match it to a beer or a new one (a new beer takes the recipe's style and targets), optionally for one location. Ingredients come in your units with their timing. Tap a recipe to see it. Recipes aren't scaled; adjust by hand.

Settings: Menu

What the taproom's menu is made of. Each beer fills these in under On the menu (Settings → Beers). Anyone with "Beer menu details" (Head brewer, Admin, and the Taproom level) sets them up.

  • Four lists: pour sizes (each with its ounces, which can be left empty), sections (groups on the menu), tags (badges like "New", and allergens and dietary notes), and your own fields (text, a number, yes / no, or picked from a list of choices you type, separated by commas).
  • Adding: tap one of the usual ones under a list (+ 16 oz, + IPAs...), or + Your own to name one yourself.
  • Changing: type a new name (renaming changes it on every beer), use ↑ and ↓ to put the list in the order the menu shows it, or Remove. A list shows how many beers use each one; removing one that's in use asks first, and takes it off those beers' menus.
  • Changes wait until Save menu; Undo changes goes back to what's saved.
  • Typed prices from before the lists existed became pour sizes, named as they were typed.

Fonts and logos

Your own files for the menu boards, kept with the rest of the brewery's records (so they're in every backup).

  • Fonts: only an Admin uploads one (Upload a font…): a WOFF2, WOFF, TTF, or OTF file up to 500 KB, with a name boards use it by. Upload only a font your brewery has the right to use on screens and on the web; check its license. Each font's name is shown in the font itself.
  • Logos: anyone who runs the boards uploads one (Upload a logo…, here or in the board builder): a PNG, JPEG, WebP, or SVG file up to 300 KB, with a name.
  • Up to 10 of each. Each shows which boards use it. A file can't be changed: Remove it (the app asks first, and says which boards will show the usual font, or no logo, instead) and upload the new one.

Menu boards

A taproom's menu boards are made from its draft lines (Inventory) and each beer's menu details. A change to a line or a beer shows on the TV within about 15 seconds, so a board is never out of date. Anyone with "Count and move finished goods" (like the Taproom level) designs and runs the boards; everyone in the brewery can preview and print them.

  • Several boards per taproom: each taproom starts with one; + Another board adds more (like "TV 1: drafts" and "TV 2: cans to go"). Each board has its own design and its own links.
  • What's on a board: each line pouring a beer or something else (its label); empty and out-of-order lines aren't shown. A beer's price at this taproom is used if it has one. Coming soon: beers in kegs in storage that aren't at this taproom yet (on a TV or paper, up to 8). To go: cases and cans on hand at this taproom.
  • Preview shows a board as the TV does and as the public page does. Print prints it on letter paper, black on white (to save ink), with its layout and fonts.

Designing a board

Design… opens the board builder: the choices on one side and a live preview on the other (on a phone, the preview is on top). Switch the preview between TV, Phone (the public page), and Paper. Nothing changes on the TV until Save board.

  • The board: its name (for you), the title on the board (empty: the taproom's name), what it shows (On tap, Coming soon, To go: at least one), and whether the beers are listed in draft line order or by section.
  • Layout: Classic list (one list; on a TV, two columns when there are many lines), Two columns (always, on a TV and on paper), Cards (each beer in its own box, with its prices underneath), or Compact (name, dotted line, price: fits the most lines).
  • What each beer shows: line number, color dot, name, tags, style, ABV, IBU, the short line (on a TV) or the description, your own fields, and prices. Untick any to hide it (the name always shows). Put them in order by dragging the ⠿ handle, or with the ↑ ↓ arrows. The number, color dot, name, and tags share the beer's first line; style, ABV, and IBU share the next (on a TV, the first). Line numbers aren't on the public page, and your own fields aren't on the TV.
  • Sections, in order: this board's order for the sections, when the beers are listed by section (otherwise the order in Settings → Menu).
  • Colors: Usual (dark TV, the phone's light or dark), Dark, Light, Chalkboard, Kraft paper, Midnight, High contrast, or My own colors (a background, text, and accent color). The builder checks how readable the colors are: easy to read across a room, readable up close, or hard to read, and asks before saving colors that are hard to read.
  • Fonts: a font pairing (a headline font for the title, sections, and beer names, and a text font): Usual, Classic, Taproom, Sign painter, Friendly, Chalk, Typewriter, Modern, or Old west; or type the name of any font from Google Fonts, or of one of your own fonts (above). If neither has a font by that name, the builder says so, and the board uses the usual font.
  • Logo: one of your logos beside the title, or none.
  • Remove board deletes a board; its links stop working right away.

A board's links

  • TV link: for the taproom's TV. It shows the short line, fills the screen with the biggest text that fits, and works without signing in. If the TV loses signal, the menu stays on screen and a small "Updated" time appears in the corner after two minutes. To set up a TV: on the computer or stick plugged into it, open the link in its web browser and make the browser full screen.
  • Public link: a phone-friendly page for your website and social posts, with each beer's description and your own fields, in the board's colors (or, with the usual colors, the phone's own light or dark). It leaves off beers marked "Leave off the public menu". Copy embed code gives the code to put the menu inside a page of your website.
  • Copy copies a link; Open opens it. New link replaces a link: the old one stops working right away (it then says so), so whatever used it needs the new one. Turn off stops it; you can make a new one later.
  • The links only ever show the menu, never volumes, batches, or people, and only the fonts and logo that board uses. A TV keeps the menu, fonts, and logo for when the signal drops. A backup keeps each board's design, and your fonts and logos, but not its links: after loading one, make the links again.

Settings: Packages

  • Package types: tick the ones you fill from the catalog (US, metric, one-way, and Cornelius kegs; casks; cases of cans and bottles; single containers), rename them, or add your own (a volume in gallons, liters, ounces, mL, imperial gallons, or barrels, times how many per package for cases). Unticking one never changes past packaging.
  • Reasons for stock changes: your list, offered on every count, move, and removal; tick Ask for a reason to require one.

Settings: Cleaning

Acid cleaning: styles that always need an acid cycle afterward (a tank is flagged "Acid due" when a batch of one of them leaves it). Each tank can also need acid every few turns (set on the tank). Log an acid cycle from the tank's settings: tap an empty tank on the board.

Settings: Team and permissions

  • Invite someone by email with a level; they get an email and join when they sign in with that address. Email again resends it; Cancel withdraws it.
  • Join code: each invite also has a code, like grist-knockout-4821, shown here and in the invite email. If they sign in with a different email, they choose Yes, I'm joining my team and type it (or you read it out). It works once, for 14 days after the invite was made or last emailed; Email again renews it. Whoever uses it joins at the invited level and shows on the team with the email they signed in with, so check the list. Five wrong codes in an hour make a person wait an hour.
  • Tap a person to change their level, adjust single permissions (the app asks: just this person, or everyone at their level), or remove them. A brewery always keeps at least one admin.
  • What each level can do: admins can change what a level includes, or reset to the usual; see Who can do what.

Settings: Alerts

  • Each kind of alert can be on or off, with its threshold: No gravity logged (after how many days, in which stages), Too long in a stage (a limit per stage; empty for none), Acid due, Under par, Low on a raw material (below the item's reorder level), Gravity step due (a logged gravity reached a step in the beer's schedule; see Planning calendar), and Short for a planned brew (below).
  • Email to: tick the people who get each kind. Nobody is ticked to start, so alerts only show on the board until you choose.
  • Short for a planned brew: on by default; look ahead how many days for it (14 to start; people who can plan the schedule change it). Items that take longer to arrive are warned about that much earlier.
  • Quiet hours: no emails between these hours (your brewery's time zone); they're sent when the quiet hours end.

Settings: Import

  1. Choose what's in it: tanks, beers, batches in tanks, cellar log entries, or a calendar plan (a row per item: the day, or rough words like "Fall" for a someday plan; what, like "Brew" or your own words; the tank, beer, who, and notes). (Import tanks and beers first, then batches, then cellar logs.)
  2. Paste cells copied from Excel or Google Sheets (with the header row), choose a CSV file, or paste a Google Sheets link (shared as "anyone with the link can view") and tap Read sheet.
  3. Check the columns (matched by name; change any) and the preview: each row is imported, already here (skipped), or a problem (and why). Numbers are read in your units; dates as 2026-10-07, 10/7/2026, or 10/7/26.
  4. Tap Import. Importing the same sheet again is safe: what's already here is skipped. New locations (for tanks) and beers (for batches) are made on the way.

Settings: Backup and export

  • Backup: Download saves everything in the brewery as one file. Load… brings a backup into an empty brewery, all at once: if anything in the file can't be loaded, nothing is, and the app says why. (The server also backs up every night.)
  • Export as spreadsheets: each list as a CSV file that opens in Excel or Google Sheets, with names instead of codes and your units in the column names, or Everything as one zip file.

Settings: API keys

A key lets an outside tool or an AI assistant read and write here as you, with some or all of your permissions (never more). Make one with a name, untick what it shouldn't do, choose how it changes things, and copy it: it's shown once. Revoke it any time; it stops working at once. Admins see every key in the brewery and when each was last used. Everything a key writes is marked on the record as "via" the key's name (in a batch's cellar log, additions, and history, and in Inventory's recent changes), and listed under What keys did lately. What the API can do: the API guide.

  • Suggests changes for a person to approve (the usual choice, and the one for AI assistants): nothing the key sends is saved by itself. It's checked (something that couldn't be saved is refused right away), then waits on the tank board under changes to review. Approve saves it as you, with your permissions, marked "via" the key and dated the day it was suggested; Reject sets it aside. Anyone who can change something here (not a viewer) can decide.
  • Makes changes directly: for a tool you trust, like a sensor or a script. Its changes are saved at once (still marked "via" the key).
  • The key list shows which each key does; What keys did lately shows each suggestion's fate (waiting, approved by, rejected by).
  • Undo (in What keys did lately) takes back a key's change, as you and under your permissions: what it added is removed (a cellar log entry, an addition, an acid cycle, a calendar item, a delivery, an order, a recipe), and what it changed is put back (a tank's status, a beer, a draft line), unless someone changed it again since; then undo says so and changes nothing. Volumes and stock (batches, transfers, packaging, level checks, moves, counts), brew-day sheet values, and raw material counts are corrected the usual way, with a level check, a count, a move, or a new value, so their history is kept.

Settings: My account

The calendar in your phone: Make a calendar link (tick Only what I'm on for just yours) and subscribe to it: Add to Apple Calendar on an iPhone or Mac, or in Google Calendar on a computer, Other calendars → + → From URL. It shows the plan and the expected steps, read-only (change plans in Brewery OS). The link is shown once; anyone with it can see the plan, so keep it to yourself. Turn off stops it at once. Google refreshes subscribed calendars only every several hours.

The email you're signed in with, switching breweries (if you're in more than one; the app remembers your choice), and Sign out. Signing out clears this phone's copy of the data; if there are changes not yet sent, the app warns you first.

Problem reports: when something goes wrong in the app (not a mistake you can fix, like a name that's already used), it sends a short report so it gets fixed: the error message, the screen you were on, the app's version, your browser, and who and which brewery. It never sends your records. With no signal, the report waits on the phone and goes later.
